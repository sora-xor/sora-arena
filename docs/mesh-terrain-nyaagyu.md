# Nyaagyu Estate mesh-terrain source

The original Blender source at `Content/SourceArt/Environment/NyaagyuTerrain/Nyaagyu_Estate_MeshTerrain_Source.blend` covers **6,400 × 5,200 cm** in the existing Nyaagyu Estate map, X from −3,200 to +3,200 cm and Y from −2,600 to +2,600 cm. The estate's gameplay ground is still the `Ground` box in `SoraWorldBuilder.cpp`; the visual tiles add no gameplay collision. The raised `CentralRoad` blockout no longer spawns in this map because it covered the imported stone path. The terrain surface lies about **1.1–3.9 cm** above Z=0, including two cosmetic irrigation-bank lips. It preserves the nearly flat traversable surface and does not cut trenches, create holes, change arena cover, or move objectives.

The editable `.blend` has 4 × 4 tile objects at each of two densities. Each tile covers **1,600 × 1,300 cm**. `SM_NyaagyuTerrain_HI_X<0–3>_Y<0–3>.fbx` samples at 12.5 cm and totals **425,984 triangles** across 16 tiles. `SM_NyaagyuTerrain_MAC_X<0–3>_Y<0–3>.fbx` samples at 50 cm and totals **26,624 triangles**. The finer conventional Mac grid makes the path edge less blocky while retaining one quarter of the high-density samples on each axis. Each FBX has local ±800 × ±650 cm X/Y bounds; `manifest.json` gives its intended world-center placement in centimeters and its SHA-256. Import either tier, never both at once. Tile `(0,0)` is placed at **(−2400, −1950, 0) cm**; each step in X adds 1600 cm and each step in Y adds 1300 cm. Both densities exactly match across tile borders in the source and in independent FBX re-import checks.

There are two stable material slots: `M_Terrain_Soil_PolyHaven` for the brown mud and leaf surface, and `M_Terrain_Stone_PolyHaven` for the central path. They map to terrain-only instances `/Game/Generated/Environment/MI_Nyaagyu_TerrainSoil` and `/Game/Generated/Environment/MI_Nyaagyu_TerrainStone`; buildings retain their original material instances. The Blender source links the project's [Poly Haven palette](../Content/SourceArt/Environment/PolyHaven/manifest.json) at the recorded 130 cm soil and 185 cm stone source scales; that palette records the imported color, normal, and ARM maps and its [CC0 license](https://polyhaven.com/license). A separately hash-pinned [stone-floor displacement scan](https://api.polyhaven.com/files/stone_floor) from the same CC0 asset gives the path shallow, texture-matched relief. The stone/soil border varies gently along the estate axis. Both terrain tiers carry world-space `EstateMacroTint` vertex colors at 4–16 m scales to reduce visible scan repetition without another texture fetch. The FBXs contain slot names, geometry, and vertex colors, with no machine-specific texture paths. The Unreal palette uses world-aligned textures and DirectX normal maps; the Blender preview inverts green solely for its renderer. The aerial and [eye-height review PNG](../Content/SourceArt/Environment/NyaagyuTerrain/Nyaagyu_Estate_StonePath_Detail_Blender.png) are Blender renders, not UE screenshots or performance results. Regularity in the broad path and soil remains visible in these source-only reviews and needs further in-engine art direction.

## UE 5.8 integration boundary

Epic describes [Mesh Terrain as experimental](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/mesh-terrain-in-unreal-engine), and [requires a World Partition level for Mesh Partition](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/crafting-mesh-terrain-in-unreal-engine). The current map is generated in an ordinary world builder; merely importing these FBXs does **not** create a Mesh Partition, World Partition cells, or runtime compiled sections. For a Mesh Terrain research level, create a World Partition/Open World level, enable `MeshPartition`, `MeshPartitionWater`, `PCG MeshPartition Interop`, and `Mesh Terrain Mode` as [Epic specifies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/accessing-mesh-terrain-in-unreal-engine), import/place the high-density tiles from the manifest, and test conversion into a partition definition. Epic says preview sections are editor-only; **Build Mesh Partitions** is required to make [compiled runtime sections](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/crafting-mesh-terrain-in-unreal-engine). Validate the resulting alignment, materials, collision, cook, streaming, and load on UE **5.8.2** before treating that path as shippable.

For the Windows art path, the high-density tier is a [Nanite candidate](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes), not a measured optimization. On the project's **M1 Ultra**, Epic lists Nanite/VSM support as requiring [Apple Silicon M2+](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine); use the conventional Mac tier and [software Lumen](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine) only after confirming rendering and performance. Thin single-sided ground meshes may have poor software-Lumen distance-field representation, so visual QA must check GI and shadows instead of assuming parity.

The [Sensei Terrain product page](https://www.unrealsensei.com/asset/senseiterrainmaterial.html) describes a UE5.8 Mesh Terrain master material and offers an email download; the creator's [Gumroad listing](https://unrealsensei.gumroad.com/l/nqrfv) lists `$25+` and a `UNREAL5` 100%-off code. Its public pages do not state a product-specific commercial/redistribution license; the site's [general terms](https://www.unrealsensei.com/terms-conditions.html) are restrictive. Sensei Terrain is therefore **not bundled or copied into this repository**. The project-owned materials and verified CC0 Poly Haven textures are the current qualified source path.

Regenerate with `blender -b --factory-startup --python scripts/art/create_nyaagyu_mesh_terrain.py` and check with `blender -b Content/SourceArt/Environment/NyaagyuTerrain/Nyaagyu_Estate_MeshTerrain_Source.blend --python scripts/art/validate_nyaagyu_mesh_terrain.py`. The validator checks 32 FBX hashes and local bounds, 7 CC0 texture hashes and relative source links, both review renders, material slots, vertex-color FBX round-trip, complete world coverage, visible path relief, high/Mac sample parity, and every internal tile seam. The Unreal runtime automation also asserts that the old blockout road cannot cover the terrain in the estate map. UE 5.8.2 import, World Partition conversion, compiled-section cook, gameplay collision, lighting, appearance, and runtime cost remain unverified. The Mac tier rose from 6,656 to 26,624 source triangles; the 60 fps target still requires a packaged M1 Ultra measurement.
