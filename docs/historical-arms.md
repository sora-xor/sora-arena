# Historical arms and campaign combat

Nyaakoto is the fictional Nyaagyū samurai protagonist, visually based on the
user-supplied grey-tabby, red-and-black armored reference. After the dojo Nyaakoto
begins each offline encounter with a katana, a yumi in inventory, and a visible
red-fletched quiver. Nyaagyū Shinkage-ryū is a fictional school in an alternate Edo setting. The
game does not teach, reproduce, or claim authorization from the living Yagyū
Shinkage-ryū. Its cinematic movements require review by a qualified Japanese
weapons historian and licensed motion performers before release.

The historical references are primary institutional sources:

- The [official Yagyūkai account of the school's characteristics](https://yagyu-shinkage-ryu.jp/?page_id=944&lang=en)
  describes natural, whole-body, responsive movement and changing one's
  approach in response to an opponent. The [Japan Kobudō Association's school
  profile](https://www.nihonkobudokyoukai.org/martialarts/031/) places the
  Yagyū line in the Edo sword tradition. Those descriptions motivate a
  committed cut, a readable attack, and recovery rather than a rapid spinning
  combo. The numeric timing and hit volume are game design values, not a claim
  about a real kata or combat rate.
- An [Edo katana in the Met collection](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/638804)
  has a 72.7 cm cutting edge and 99.8 cm overall length. The project-owned
  hero sword source uses a 77–82 cm blade section with restrained curvature,
  differentiated edge/ridge/back, tsuka, and saya. Its first-person grip and
  animation still need hand-fit and historical review.
- The [Met's 1727 foot yari](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/27609)
  notes that foot spears were commonly around seven feet. The campaign yari is
  a straight point on a long shaft with a narrow thrust volume and poor
  control if an opponent crowds inside its useful distance. Its collision
  reach includes the wielder's extension and is not a museum object dimension.
- The [All Japan Naginata Federation's description](https://naginata.jp/naginata/viewpoint.html)
  distinguishes cuts and thrusts, proper distance and timing, and *zanshin*
  after contact. The campaign naginata uses a distinct bladed pole silhouette,
  a wider committed cut and longer recovery than the yari. A complete historic
  technique set, including transitions and thrusts, is still to be authored.
- The [All Japan Kyudo Federation's shooting sequence](https://www.kyudo.jp/howto/syaho.html)
  treats stance, draw, release and follow-through as related actions. Its
  [equipment rules](https://www.kyudo.jp/pdf/schedule/taikai/20240607_tebiki.pdf)
  state a standard 221 cm asymmetric yumi with its grip about one-third from
  the lower end. The campaign bow has that silhouette and requires a held draw
  and release, then launches a visible, gravity-affected arrow instead of a
  firearm hit scan. The 650 ms game draw is not a simulation of formal kyūdō.

The offline campaign has separate yumi, yari and naginata IDs 18–20 after
the initial dojo chapter. Keyboard 4/5/6 selects them; controller right
shoulder cycles them. The first 18 Arena weapons and Rust proof-bound rules
remain unchanged. The historical arms cannot be admitted to P2P matches or
used to infer XOR or NFT entitlements. Their current generated silhouettes are
temporary geometry and have not passed visual, animation, controller feel or
historian review. The dojo currently uses an original wooden practice sword;
the fictional school should not be marketed as a demonstration of an authentic
Yagyū training practice.
