# Katana koshirae construction: Edo uchigatana, c. 1700–1850

Reviewed 2026-10-18. This is a sourced construction reference for the photoreal hero katana and saya. It records museum and expert measurements, how the parts were built, and how family crests (kamon) were applied. It does **not** approve any existing asset.

**Method and access.** Records were read from these sources:

- the Met Collection API (`/v1/objects`; the old `/v1/search` was retired on 2026-10-01, so the paginated `/v1.1/search` was used);
- the V&A API v2 and the Cleveland Museum of Art open-access API;
- Bunka Isan Online (Agency for Cultural Affairs), which mirrors records from Tokyo NM, Kyushu NM, ICU and Tokyo University of the Arts;
- Art Platform Japan (APJ, which carries ColBase data) and the Tokyo National Museum (TNM) image archive;
- the NBTHK English journal;
- Touken World (Nagoya Tōken Museum, an educational site).

Some sources could not be read:

- The Met's web pages returned HTTP 429 or a bot wall, so the Met's descriptive label text could not be read.
- The British Museum, MFA Boston, Walters, Worcester and Penn collection sites returned 403.
- The ColBase API needs a site key.

No images were downloaded.

**Conventions.**

- Dimensions are given as each source published them. The spec in §3 is in mm.
- *Omote* (sashi-omote) is the face that points away from the body when the sword is worn edge-up on the left hip.
- **(inference)** marks reasoning that no source states.

## 2. Construction notes by component

### 2.1 Measurement conventions

- **Nagasa** is the straight line from the munemachi to the kissaki tip. **Sori** is the greatest gap between the mune and that line ([JSI-measure], [TW-sori]).
- **Kissaki length** is measured from the yokote to the tip [JSI-measure].
- The Met reports "L. of cutting edge" (≈ nagasa) and "L. of blade" (blade plus nakago). Its overall "L." does not state whether the scabbard is included (§5).

### 2.2 Blade: shinogi-zukuri geometry

- **Ridge lines.** In shinogi-zukuri the shinogi-suji runs from the munemachi to the kissaki. The yokote-suji runs from the edge near the tip to the shinogi [TW-shinogi].
- **Back.** Every Edo katana record read is shinogi-zukuri; some wakizashi are flat-sided. Most have an iori-mune (two-plane peaked back) [BIO-591770], [BIO-568282], [NBTHK-745]; mitsu-mune also occurs [NBTHK-Mar].
- **Edo shapes, standard Edo.** Naotane, 1815: standard width, slightly thick, large sori in the lower half, long chū-kissaki [NBTHK-745].
- **Edo shapes, Satsuma.** Masayuki, 1807: high shinogi, fairly deep sori, wide, thick, "平肉豊か" (full, convex hira-niku), chū-kissaki slightly long [BIO-591770]. Satsuma shinshintō often shows "prominent sori at the koshimoto" [NBTHK-Mar].
- **Edo shapes, Mito.** Thick, wide, little width taper, shallow sori, long chū-kissaki, with wide shinogi-ji and a high shinogi [NBTHK-Mar].
- **Edo shapes, Sa Yukihide (1851).** Wide, slightly thin, with an ō-kissaki [BIO-568282].
- **Proportions measured.** Motohaba 2.9–3.2 cm; sakihaba 2.0 cm; motokasane 0.65–0.75 cm; sakikasane 0.35–0.5 cm; kissaki 3.8–3.85 cm ([NBTHK-745], [BIO-544411], [BIO-156251], [NBTHK-Mar] kantei 830).
- **Nakago.** In the unshortened Met blades and [NBTHK-745], nakago are 20.4–22.0 cm long (Met: blade length minus cutting edge). Edo ubu nakago have one mekugi-ana [BIO-591770], with kurijiri or iriyamagata tips. Their file marks are katte-sagari or katte-agari, sometimes with keshō [BIO-568282], [BIO-591770].
- **(inference)** Place the shinogi about ⅓ of the width from the mune. Make the hira-ji gently convex toward a slightly rounded edge (hamaguri-like). Leave the mune about 1 mm thinner than the kasane at the shinogi.

### 2.3 Steel surface: hada, hamon, nie and nioi, and polish zones

- **Nie and nioi.** Nie are martensite particles large enough to see individually, and look white. Nioi are too fine to see one by one and read as a continuous misty band. Ko-nie are typical of shintō and later work ([TW-niedeki], [TW-nioideki], [TW-konie]).
- **Edo example, 1807.** Ko-itame, tightly forged, with thick ji-nie and fine chikei. Hamon of notare mixed with gunome, deep nioiguchi, thick nie, sunagashi and kinsuji [BIO-591770].
- **Edo example, 1851.** Wide hamon of shallow notare with ō-gunome and long ashi, and a bright nioiguchi [BIO-568282].
- **Polish (togi).** It is an Important Intangible Cultural Property. It shapes the sugata and niku and brings out the colour and pattern of the steel. Work runs from arato through shitaji-togi; finally the mune and shinogi-ji are burnished and the bōshi is given *narume* [BIO-273306].
- **Zones after polish.** The shinogi-ji and mune are mirror-burnished. The hira and ha are matte so the grain and hamon show. Nugui darkens the ji. Hadori whitens the hamon area, while sashikomi keeps its natural outline. The yokote line is brought out at the finishing stage [WP-polish].
- **Look for the 3D artist (partly inference).** Dark, nearly mirror shinogi-ji and mune. A blue-grey matte ji with visible ko-itame. A whitish ha with a soft-edged hamon boundary. A crisp yokote, and a whitish kissaki.
- **(inference)** The "modern" polish was completed after the Meiji period [BIO-273306]. A sword in Edo daily use may therefore show a less whitened, sashikomi-like hamon.

### 2.4 Habaki and seppa

- **Habaki function.** It floats the blade inside the saya and holds it so that it cannot slip out [TW-habaki].
- **Materials.** Early habaki were iron *tomo-habaki*. In the Edo period plain copper was the norm; daimyō blades had gold or silver. A *kise-habaki* has a thin metal sheet (foil) brazed over a copper base. Gold kise was "said to be" restricted to daimyō and upper samurai [TW-habaki].
- **Forms.** Single *ichijū* habaki are typical of shintō. Double *nijū* habaki are typical of kotō and were used in daimyō collections from the early Edo period. Domain styles include Owari, Ōsaka, Mito, Kaga, Shōnai, Higo and Satsuma [TW-habaki].
- **Museum examples.** Shakudō-covered habaki with gilt narrow sides [VA-609-1908]; copper overlaid with silver [VA-613-1908]; cast copper with a rusticated surface and each face pierced with a circle [VA-603-1908].
- **File marks.** Tachi habaki carry vertical file marks [TW-habaki]. **(inference)** Diagonal decorative file marks (collectors' "nekogaki") are common on Edo copper habaki, but no museum source confirming this was found.
- **Seppa count.** An uchigatana normally has one pair of small seppa; a tachi has 6–8 [TW-seppa].
- **Seppa materials and finish.** Copper or brass. The face carries vertical file marks, the back carries crossed file marks, and the rim is lined with a *sujime* file. The outline follows the koiguchi and fuchi [TW-seppa].
- **Seppa variants.** "Embattled" or "vandyked" edges occur [VA-613-1908], [VA-609-1908].

### 2.5 Tsuba

- **Hole and seppa-dai.** On an uchigatana tsuba the nakago-ana narrows toward the top, because the edge is worn upward; a tachi tsuba is the reverse. Copper pieces are fitted inside the hole to adjust the fit (sekigane) [TW-tosogu]. The seppa-dai is the central area under the seppa, and it carries the signature [TW-seppa].
- **Hitsu-ana.** A kōgai-hitsu and/or kozuka-hitsu may flank the nakago-ana [TW-tosogu], [VA-M.105-1911]. The kozuka handle they must pass is 9.7 × 1.4 × 0.6 cm [MET-29675](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/29675).
- **Sizes.** Most measured Edo iron tsuba here are 72–85 mm tall; one early-Edo ICU piece reaches 97.5 mm. Thickness is 3–6 mm in Met records and 4.5–7 mm in ICU records (late Edo: 4.5–6.5 mm). Met iron tsuba weigh about 85–155 g; the lightest, [MET-30005](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/30005), is 85 g.
- **Mimi (rim).** It may be thickened ("square iron guard with thickened border" [VA-629-1908](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O497296/)) or have an applied rim (a shakudō rim on [V&A M.885-1910](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O466407/)).
- **Shape.** Shapes include round, naga-maru, mokkō and aoi-gata. The shogunate banned "large tsuba" [TW-10002].

### 2.6 Tsuka (hilt)

- **Core.** Honoki, seasoned 10–20 years and usually made by the sayashi [TW-tsukamaki], [TW-tsuka]. An uchigatana tsuka is straight, not curved like a tachi hilt, and slightly waisted at the centre [TW-tsuka].
- **Samegawa (ray skin).** It is glued on with *sokui* (rice paste) [TW-tsuka]. The skin is soaked overnight to soften, polished with *uzukuri* and ibota wax, and sometimes lacquered black ([TW-tsukamaki], [TW-tsuka]). Each skin has one large *oyatsubu* nodule, and the wrap is laid to show it in a diamond [TW-tsuka]. **(inference)** Put the oyatsubu on the omote, in a diamond window between the menuki and the kashira.
- **Hishigami.** Kneaded, unprocessed washi cut about 1.5 cm wide and laid several sheets thick under each crossing, pushed in with a *kujiri* awl. It locks the menuki and shapes the diamonds [TW-tsukamaki], [TW-tsuka].
- **Ito.** In the Edo period, pure-silk braid over samegawa [TW-tsukamaki]. Other materials include deer leather, whale baleen and wisteria [TW-tsuka]. Museum colours include fine black silk [VA-628-1908], fine blue silk [VA-613-1908], dark blue ([V&A 673&A-1908](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O463684/)) and two shades of brown ([VA-635-1908](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O497294/)). **(inference)** Width 9–10 mm; no museum record states ito width.
- **Wrap.** It runs from the edge of the fuchi to below the kashira [TW-tsukamaki]. Styles:
  - hira-maki: flat, used on tachi;
  - hishi-maki: diamond, the most common;
  - katate-maki: spiral;
  - **tsumami-maki**: pinched raised crossings, "often seen on uchigatana";
  - **hineri-maki**: a half-twist at each crossing;
  - jabara-maki;
  - **kake-maki**: the ito passes over the kashira instead of through its *shitodome* holes [TW-tsukamaki].
- **Diamond count. (inference)** About 9 diamonds per face on a 245 mm tsuka with 9–10 mm ito at roughly 24 mm pitch. No source states a standard count.
- **Menuki.** Set on the samegawa and wrapped under the ito; they improve the grip [TW-koshirae], [TW-tsukamaki]. They sit on the centreline, giving the term *menuki-dōri* [TW-tosogu], and are about 3 cm across [TW-menuki]. Measured Edo menuki are 2.9–3.8 cm long (§1c).
- **Menuki placement.** On an edge-up uchigatana the omote menuki is offset toward the fuchi, the ura menuki toward the kashira, and animal heads face the fuchi. This is stated by Takeuchi citing Suzuki (1995) [NMB-menuki]. Reversed *gyaku-menuki* is a feature of Yagyū koshirae [TW-koshirae].
- **Mekugi.** Katana normally have one peg [WP-mountings]. In shirasaya the peg is bamboo or water-buffalo horn, 2–3 cm long [JAWP-saya].
- **Fuchi and kashira.** The fuchi covers the start of the wrap and the kashira ends it. Materials include horn, iron, brass and soft metals [TW-fuchigashira]. Measured Edo fuchi are 3.8–4.0 × 2.2–2.65 cm, with a band height of 1.2–1.5 cm. Kashira are 3.4–3.9 × 1.6–2.0 cm, with a depth of 0.8–1.15 cm (§1d; **(inference)** reading TUA "厚" as band height or cap depth).

### 2.7 Saya and sageo

- **Wood.** Honoki; earlier also sugi or hinoki. It is cut to fit the blade, hollowed out with chisels and lacquered. *Maru-zaya* were usual from the Muromachi period through the Edo period [TW-tosogu].
- **Halves.** The two halves are joined with rice-paste glue and can be split at the seam for cleaning [JAWP-saya].
- **Finishes.** *Ishime* or *tataki* (textured, for grip) and *ro-nuri* (smooth gloss) [JAWP-saya]. Museum examples:
  - black roiro [BIO-175384];
  - black ishime [BIO-517362];
  - "roughened black lacquer" ([VA-609-1908], [VA-632-1908]);
  - ribbed (*inrō-kizami*) [MET-24873], [VA-M.341-1940];
  - togidashi same [MET-24629].
- **Colour rules.** The shogunate banned vermilion and yellow saya [TW-10002], although a vermilion Satsuma saya survives [VA-613-1908].
- **Fittings.** The koiguchi and kojiri are traditionally water-buffalo horn [WP-mountings]. Examples: a black horn rim-mount, kojiri and kurikata [VA-629-1908]; iron or shibuichi mounts ([VA-609-1908], [VA-613-1908]). The kojiri is often reinforced with metal [JAWP-saya]. The *kaerizuno* is a hook that locks the saya against the obi while drawing [WP-mountings], [JAWP-uchigatana].
- **Hitsu.** The kōgai sits on the saya omote and the kozuka on the ura, the face against the body [TW-tosogu]. A wakizashi in a daishō often omits the kōgai [TW-tosogu].
- **Sageo.** Silk (also leather and ramie, later cotton), flat-braided; *kai-no-kuchi* braid is the most common. It passes through the kurikata on the sashi-omote. In practical wear it is untied and wrapped on the obi, tucked in, or hung loose around the saya; decorative knots are wrong for use [JAWP-sageo]. Length is "commonly accepted" as 5 shaku (about 150 cm); Tenshin-ryū uses about 6 shaku, and no historical regulation was found [TSR-sageo].

### 2.8 How it was worn

- The katana was "thrust cutting edge uppermost through the sash" [VA-M.48-1971](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O90838/). The tachi hung edge-down from two hangers [WP-mountings], [VA-M.139-1929](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O71075/).
- Edo samurai commonly wore it in *otoshi-zashi* (sloped down) [JAWP-uchigatana].
- It was worn as a daishō with the wakizashi [MET-638804]. Commoners were barred from wearing a daishō [TW-koshirae].

## 5. Uncertainties and conflicts

- **Met "L."** It is not stated whether it includes the scabbard. Overall length minus cutting edge ranges from 26.6 to 39.9 cm across the Met records, so the tsuka length of 245 mm is derived, not published.
- **Met 638804 vs 21889.** Blade 93.9 vs 93.8 cm; curvature 3.1 vs 3.0 cm. The daishō wakizashi curvature of 0.36 cm looks anomalous.
- **Met 30092.** The weight is internally inconsistent: 4 oz is not 10.2 g.
- **Met crest content.** The Met's label text was inaccessible, so the crest form of menuki 29466 and 35005 is unverified. No crest-form menuki **with dimensions** was confirmed.
- **V&A lengths.** FE.2-1974 "total 133 cm" against a 72 cm blade is implausible. 609&A gives 111.8 cm against a 74.3 cm blade. V&A length conventions vary between records.
- **V&A M.139.** Its two records date the same tachi assembly differently: "about 1850" and "around 1750–1800".
- **NBTHK-745.** The numbers come from search-tool extraction of the NBTHK PDF; the PDF itself could not be parsed here.
- **Kasane.** It is defined at the mune in the Wikipedia glossary, but sellers often measure at the shinogi ([NMB-kasane]).
- **Menuki placement.** It is a convention (Suzuki, 1995, via [NMB-menuki]). Gyaku-menuki existed, and forum experts say placement became the owner's preference.
- **Gold habaki restriction.** Hedged as "と言われています" ("it is said") [TW-habaki]. The ban on vermilion saya [TW-10002] conflicts with a surviving vermilion Satsuma saya [VA-613-1908].
- **Sageo length.** 5 vs 6 shaku [TSR-sageo]. The Edo sword-length limits cited by [JAWP-uchigatana] are secondary and class-specific.
- **Definitions.** Nie and nioi are defined differently by [WP-hamon] and Touken World; Touken World's usage is followed.
- **Thickness readings.** The Met "thickness" for tsuba may be taken at the rim or at the seppa-dai. The TUA "厚" for fuchi-kashira is read as band height **(inference)**.
- **Unconfirmed attributions.** TNM F-19985 has no dimensions or blade link in the image record. [BIO-579326](https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/579326) (koshirae of the National Treasure Sukezane tachi, 19th c.) names no holding museum.
- **Inferences.** Every number marked **(inference)** in §3 still needs checking against a measured physical example or a curator.
