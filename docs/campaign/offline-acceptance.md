# Offline campaign progression acceptance

## Campaign data (catalog schema 4)

The chapter scripts in `docs/campaign/chapter-0N.md` are the authored source. Each carries one YAML block written to the story bible's beat taxonomy (§12.1, §12.2) and chapter-YAML conventions (§12.5). `python3 scripts/unreal/build_campaign_data.py` turns block N into `Content/Data/campaign/chapter_0N.json`; `--check` fails when a committed file differs from a fresh build. The importer needs PyYAML (`pip3 install --user -r scripts/requirements.txt`). It rejects duplicate YAML keys, non-JSON values, keys outside the §12 contract without an `x_` prefix, unknown beat, task, ride, defeat, scoring and dialogue-mode values, registers with no §18 family, times finer than a millisecond, fractions finer than a permille, `fail` rows the runtime cannot act on, and dialogue conditions that are neither `collectible:<id>` nor `story:<flag>`. Its self-test must reject 15 bad inputs, among them a horse mounted from the left and a matchlock in the saddle. Each chapter file holds:

- `beats`: the §12.1 common fields, always present with fixed types and their defaults filled in (`requires` defaults to the previous beat, `fail` to `player_died` → `checkpoint`); the §12.2 parameters under `params`, with completion normalized to `{kind, …}`; the beat's `task`; and every `x_` proposal, without its prefix, under `ext`. `unlocks.grants` names a skipped optional weapon a later beat grants (weapon 13 at ch07.b01), which is not a second unlock. `unlocks.story_flags` names a story flag the beat sets (ch07.b05's `x_story_flag`, `nafudakake_marked`).
- `task`: the beat's §12.2 parameters in the integer units `SoraBeatRules.h` runs, with the same keys on every beat: the goal (`elapsed`, `lines`, `waves_cleared`, `objectives`, `survive`, `points`, `lessons`, `opponent_down`, `exit`, `hold`, `turns`, `escort`, `deliveries`, `steps`, `cache`), its count and duration in milliseconds, the waves (trigger, value in ms or permille, the event that releases a volume or alarm wave, and spawn groups with their lethal and nonlethal counts), boss and duel phases in health permille, lesson forms, the loss policy, the hold's contest and decay, the crank's rate band as turn intervals, the escort's pace, reach and route, pickups, drop-offs, manufacturing steps with their rhythm and tolerance, defended targets, `on_detected` and its reinforcement wave, `on_horse_down`, and the `fail` rows as failures. The loader reads this block; the host driver plays it.
- `lines`: all 1,032 voiced lines once, with speaker id, `duration_s`, the register's §18 family and the alias's delivery note.
- `triggers`: the one table that schedules lines, as `{beat, on, mode, lines, requires_flag}` rows. It covers beat dialogue entries, dialogue beats, boss and duel barks, cinematic subtitles (with `at_ms` on the cinematic's own clock) and the ch05/ch06 bark pools. No line has an absolute start time. Where a script lists a cinematic's subtitles without times (ch05–ch07), the importer spreads them over the cinematic in script order without overlap and marks each row `ext.at_ms_spread`. `requires_flag` holds a conditional line back until its collectible is found or its story flag is set (ch08.b17's returned Washio sword waits for `collectible:ch08.c03`).
- `sites`, `checkpoints` (in target centiseconds, with per-phase saves), `unlocks`, `collectibles`, `ui_prompts`, `places` (from the bible's §14 table, each marked `hub` when its row is a bold "**Arena N**") and `layout` (ch01–ch03's volumes, points, splines and patrols in integer centimetres, in each chapter's authored frame; ch04–ch08 have none). The authored layout is kept for the level artists; the runtime plays `space`.
- `space`: the playable layout `scripts/unreal/campaign_space.py` derives by rule from the places, beats and tasks, in integer centimetres in the Unreal world frame (origin at the arena centre, every floor at z 0). See **Chapter spaces** below.
- Carry tasks also carry `carry_speed_permille` (the script's `move_speed_mult`) and `carry_weapons` (`katana_one_hand` → the katana, else nothing), and an escort's `path_cm` is the length of its road.

`Content/Data/campaign/campaign.json` is hand-authored: the chapter files, the chapter title-card narrative and objective, the three production-site labels, the mechanic flags (with their Japanese and English names) and the story flags in save-bit order, the collectible categories, and bilingual labels for the manufacturing steps and the defended targets the HUD names. `Content/Data/catalog.json` keeps the shared core (classes, weapons, maps, hero) and the 40 dialogue speakers. `SoraArena.Build.cs` stages `Content/Data/...`, which UnrealBuildTool's file filter resolves recursively.

`python3 scripts/unreal/validate_content.py` requires the committed chapter files to equal a fresh build and runs `scripts/unreal/validate_campaign.py`, which checks the bible's §12.3 rules: pacing against §10 (the beats sum to exactly 25,200 s), sites 0–2 once per chapter in `requires` order, weapons 0–20 once each at their §11 beats in §11 order, bosses from §8 (and `bosses.json` once it exists), mutō-dori attacks only where the fight's defeat or finisher allows them, mounted weapons within {0, 18, 19}, registry entries only on production tasks and cargo rides, every line bilingual with a catalog speaker within the subtitle limits, nonlethal enemies never counted toward a defeat count, and at most 360 target seconds between checkpoints. It also checks line references both ways, blocking dialogue of at most 20 s, collectibles and prompts, the 25 schema-3 cues verbatim, every story flag set by exactly one beat, every conditional line's collectible or flag provided by an earlier find or beat, and that each task has a goal and counts the same lethal opponents as its waves. It checks every chapter's space: zones and road corridors never overlap each other or the arena; every beat's entry, anchor, objectives, pickups, drop-offs, targets, stations, spawns, patrol posts, cache, horse and every loose collectible stand on their zone's floor clear of its walls and cover (in the hub, of the arena's buildings, cover, lantern poles and torii posts, mirrored per map by `campaign_space.arena_blockers`); every volume a beat's events name is placed in its zone; rides and escorts run on roads as long as their scripts say, and an escort walks its whole road; every manufacturing step has its station; horses have rising gait speeds, the katana, yumi and yari only, and a yumi arc to the left; and every mechanic, step and target has a bilingual label. Its self-test passes the real data, then must reject 29 mutated variants. The glyph check covers every campaign string against the bundled Noto Sans CJK JP font.

## Chapter spaces

`campaign_space.py` lays out each chapter in one world frame:

- **The hub**: the chapter's own arena (Nyaagyu Estate, Lantern Ward, Silk Foundry or Debt Citadel), built by `SoraWorldBuilder.cpp` exactly as the training arena is, when one of the chapter's places is the arena itself. Hub beats use the arena's clear road, its three production sites, its caches, six invader spawns clear of every arena's buildings, nooks for collectibles and two patrol loops. Chapter 4 has no hub.
- **Zones**: a walled floor for every other place, by form (bible §14): halls 36 × 26 m with 7 m walls, yards 44 × 32 m, fields 72 × 52 m; a zone hosting a boss is at least 56 × 42 m. Each has an entry, cover by its long walls where a fight happens, lantern posts in its corners, production sites along its far wall, work spots in its middle, spawns at its edges, two patrol loops, a cache corner and collectible nooks. Estate yards wear the authored four-metre wall module; storehouses, halls, pines, trees and street or alien buildings stand outside the walls as backdrop, so no visual-only prop covers a work spot.
- **Roads**: every ride and escort gets a straight walled corridor 24 m wide with a packed-earth road swaying ±4.5 m inside it, as long as the script says (`route_length_m`, `path_length_m`, or ch01's `spl_ch01_umaba_lane` at 300 m) or as its pace and target time imply. The road's trigger volumes stand at their authored chainage (the ch01 pheasant at 150 m) or spread along it, spawns line its walls, and the exit fills its end.
- Every beat's record gives its zone, its entry (where a travel cut puts the hero), its anchor and points, its volumes (every `volume_enter:` its waves, lines, closing or exit, triggers, scripted spooks and forced-gait stretches name), its exit, spawns, patrols, cache and cinematic camera (a dolly from one mark to another looking at the place), the task's kit (`sluice`, `bellows` or `lift` for a crank by its actor, `hold`, `stations`, `carry` with the item's kind, `defend` with each target's kind, `escort` with its party), and for rides and mounted fights the horse: coat, whether the ride starts in the saddle, the tack to put on first (ch01.b06), gaits and their speeds (chapter-04 §6.5, overridden by a script's own), wind per gait, the gallop lockout, fear radius and recovery, scripted spooks, gait caps along the road and forced-gait volumes, weapons and their arcs, whether the rider may dismount, the phase a mounted fight is dismounted at, and its pack horses (a convoy on the road ahead, or a laden mare on a lead rope).
- Loose collectibles (those no explore cache holds) get a pickup in their place's zone, or beside the road near its start when the hero only passes through the place.

`FSoraCatalog::LoadSpace` (`SoraCatalogSpace.cpp`) reads the block fail-closed: every field typed, point counts equal to the beat's task, an escort's path equal to its road, a cinematic's camera, a dialogue's closing anchor, an explore beat's cache, every weapon arc, and every horse `SoraMountRules::HorseValid` accepts. A collectible must lie in exactly one cache or at one pickup.

## Beat rules

`SoraBeatRules.h` holds the chapter gates (requirements, sites, weapon, mechanic and story-flag unlocks, checkpoints, entry masks) and the beat engine: an `FTask` per beat and the pure functions that play it, in integer milliseconds, centimetres and permille.

| Type | Completion | Advance | Failure and retry |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cinematic` | `duration_s` on its clock, or skipped | `Tick`, `Skip` | a fall returns to the checkpoint |
| `dialogue` | its lines played, then its closing anchor reached when it has one | `FinishLines`, `Reach` | as above |
| `combat_wave` | every wave but a reinforcement released and every lethal opponent down; or its objectives worked; or `survive_s` survived | `ReleaseWaves` (start, prev_cleared, alive_below, after_s, progress_at, then volume and event waves by `ReleaseWave`), `RecordDefeat`, `WorkObjective` | its `fail` rows |
| `duel` | lesson forms then bouts; first to N points; or the opponent's health to zero | `RecordForms`, `ScorePoint`, `OpponentHealth` (phases at their health) | a sparring bout lost is the rival's point; a lost match retries in place (`RetryTask` keeps losses and forms) or, under `continue_after:N`, moves the story on after N losses; a lost lesson bout retries in place |
| `boss` | the boss's health to zero | `OpponentHealth` through each phase | phase checkpoints restore at the phase's health |
| `stealth_zone` | the exit reached, or its objectives worked; its alarms never have to fall | `Travel`, `Reach`, `WorkObjective` | detection follows `on_detected`: combat, a checkpoint restart, or the named reinforcement wave |
| `production_task` hold | the hold's time spent in its volume | `Tick` (contest pauses or decays; work under guard pauses while seen) | its `fail` rows |
| · crank | N turns | `Turn`: faster than the band slips and restarts the rhythm; a slack crank decays | as above |
| · escort | the party covers its route | `Tick` while the hero stays within reach | a fallen escort (`fail_on_npc_death`) |
| · carry | N deliveries | `Pickup`, `Deliver` (each drop-off takes an equal share), `DropCarried` on a hit where the carry drops | as above |
| · manufacture | every step in order | `Stroke`: an off-beat stroke restarts its rhythm step | as above |
| · defend | `duration_s` survived, or the waves cleared | `DamageTarget`, `RepairTarget` | a destroyed target (`fail_on_target_destroyed`) |
| `horseback_ride` | the destination, or every cargo drop-off | `Travel` (ambushes come as the rider passes their mark), `Reach`, `Deliver` | `on_horse_down`: checkpoint or on foot |
| `explore_optional` | its cache opened | `OpenCache` | — |
| `collectible` | picked up | `Find` | — |

Every beat that clears its waves (all but stealth zones) completes only once every released lethal opponent is down. `TaskCheckpointValid` allows a phase checkpoint only in a boss, or a duel fought to its end, below its phase count. `TasksValid` refuses a chapter that does not open on a cinematic, an optional beat that is not an explore beat, or a required explore beat.

Run `python3 scripts/unreal/test_campaign_progression.py` from the repository root. It generates a temporary C++ fixture of every beat's gates and task and the §11 unlock order, and compiles `campaign_progression_driver.cpp` (C++17, `-Wall -Wextra -Werror -pedantic`) against `SoraBeatRules.h`, `SoraCampaignRules.h` and `SoraDojoRules.h`. The script also requires `SoraCampaignDirector.cpp` to call the tested rules and `SoraCatalog.cpp` to refuse chapters by `ChapterValid` and `TasksValid`. The driver:

- runs every beat type's completion, advance, failure, retry and checkpoint rules on hand-made tasks, and the parameters `TaskValid` must refuse;
- plays every one of the 133 authored tasks to completion as a hero who does everything right (so no authored wave or goal can deadlock), and restores each phase checkpoint and plays it out;
- puts every authored task through a fall, its `fail` rows, lost matches, detection and a lost horse;
- plays every chapter twice, skipping or playing the optional beats, checking gates, checkpoints and forged saves (an unearned site, weapon, mechanic or story flag, a beat saved without its requirements, a premature completion), and the carried entry masks between chapters;
- requires the completist to hold every weapon at its §11 beat in §11 order and the skipper to differ only by the optional pickup the ch07.b01 grant restores; both end with all 21 weapons and 19 mechanics, the completist with the story flag;
- keeps the chapter-advance, collectibles, training-arena and chapter-one dojo lesson tests.

`SoraBeatRules::SiegeDamage` wears a defended target down by `SiegeHpPerSecond` (5) for every opponent within `SiegeRadiusCm` (7 m), carried in whole opponent-milliseconds so no frame loses a fraction. `SoraMountRules.h` is the horseback rule set: with the legs on (W) a halted horse walks on and holds her gait; urging (Shift) asks for the next gait every 700 ms up to the ride's fastest, the road's cap and a walk while she is spooked; gathered reins (S) drop a gait every 400 ms; left alone she eases a gait every 1.5 s. Wind rises or falls per gait each second; a gallop below 20 locks her into a canter until she recovers 50. A spook forces a walk for the ride's recovery time. `CanMount` accepts a rider only at her right flank (40–220 cm to her right, within 140 cm of the saddle), `AtLeftFlank` names the refused side, and `ArcAllows` lets the katana, yumi and yari act only in their arcs of her heading (yumi −170°…+15°, katana +30°…+150°, yari −10°…+60° unless the script sets its own); nothing else acts from the saddle.

The driver also runs the siege's whole-point carry and the carry pace and loadout rules, every mount rule (walk on, urging steps and ceilings, reins, easing, spooks, forced walks, the gallop's wind and lockout, the right-side mount, the arcs and the invalid horses `HorseValid` refuses), and rides every authored route at its best pace through its gait caps. A route must be ridden to its end within its target time, or within half as long again where the script's own route length outruns its pacing estimate; it prints each overrun (ch02.b10: 133 s against 120 s; ch02.b13: 127 s against 90 s).

Ten mutations of the rules (no stalled-field release, hurried crank turns counting, no concession after the last loss, flags not earned, reinforcements required, phases off their health, rhythm never missing, waves not cleared, retries forgetting losses, any detection restarting) each fail the driver.

## Unreal runtime (schema 4, save schema 6)

`FSoraCatalog::Load` reads `catalog.json`, `campaign/campaign.json` and the eight chapter files and fails closed: a missing or mistyped field, an unknown speaker, line, beat, mechanic, story flag or collectible category, a task that `TaskValid` refuses, a chapter that `ChapterValid` or `TasksValid` refuses, a subtitle outside a cinematic, a conditional line naming an unknown collectible or flag, or checkpoint rows that disagree with a beat leave the catalog unloaded with the reason in `FSoraCatalog::Error`. Every route through the eight chapters must end with all 21 weapons and all 19 mechanics.

`ASoraWorldBuilder::Build` builds a chapter from its space (`SoraWorldBuilderLayout.cpp`, `SoraWorldBuilderLayoutZones.cpp`): the hub arena (its own sites left out), every zone and road with floors, walls, cover and dressing, the chapter's lighting, and the three production sites at their space's positions with their kind's structure. `PlayerStart` is the first beat's entry. The training arena is built as before, and kernel practice keeps its own map.

`USoraCampaignDirector` plays each chapter's beats in the order `SoraBeatRules` allows and each beat's task through the same functions (`SoraCampaignDirector.cpp`), with its world-facing play in `SoraCampaignDirectorWorld.cpp`. It keeps an integer beat clock. When a beat starts in another place than the hero, a travel cut (a short fade) puts him at its entry; rides and escorts start on their road. The hero entering a trigger volume raises its event once per beat: its lines play, a dialogue waiting on it starts, the waves it names come, and on a ride a scripted spook startles the horse. Waves spawn at the beat's spawns (on a road, the ones 25–65 m ahead of the hero) up to `max_alive`; stealth guards stand at their patrol route's start and `GuardPatrol(Guard)` gives the AI lane each guard's route. `Travel` tracks the way to an exit along the road, or across the zone. A gold beacon marks the active point: the next objective, pickup or drop-off, the current step's station, the defended position, the escorted party, the next wave's volume, or the exit. E works the nearest point within reach (from the saddle, 4.2 m), then a loose collectible or an explore cache, then the horse.

The task kits (`SoraWorldBuilderLayoutTasks.cpp`) show each production task as it goes: a sluice winch whose drum turns while the weir board lifts and the water rises in its channel; bellows whose furnace mouth glows brighter (with its light); an override wheel or capstan lifting a platform of goods; a ring on the ground that fills as ground is held (red while contested); a bench per manufacturing station, the current step's in gold and finished ones in teal, with the output stacking beside it; loads at each pickup (a pack horse, or a stack that empties) and what each drop-off has received; each defended target (pack horse, furnace, aid tent or villager) with a banner that pales to red as it is worn down; and the escorted party (villagers on the samurai cat rig, walking or standing, and ch04's ox cart) walking the road where the rules put them. A carried load slows the hero to its pace (no sprint), shows in his arms and leaves at most the katana in hand; other weapons are refused with a notice. Opponents standing at a defended target wear it down (`SiegeDamage`), and a destroyed target fails the defence to its checkpoint. Loose collectibles lie in their zones as small period objects with a faint glow; picking one up goes to the codex and the save at once.

Horses (`ASoraHorse`, `SoraHorse.cpp`) are native Japanese horses about 130 cm at the withers with period tack: a lacquered kura on front and rear arches, aori flaps, the girth, broad abumi on both sides with the SORA mon on their front plates, the bit, headstall, cloth tazuna and crupper; pack horses carry a 荷鞍 with two loads. Their body is a primitive proxy in the coat's colour (chestnut Kurimaru, bay pack horses): no horse mesh is imported into `Content/Generated`, only the source-only anatomy study. Legs swing in each gait's footfall order as placeholders for an equine animator's cycles. A ride's horse waits at the road's start: ch01.b06's stands bare, and E at her side puts on the kura, girth, abumi and bit in order, the bit refused twice until she has her barley; E at her right flank mounts, at her left is refused with a notice. The other rides and ch04.b16's mounted first phase start in the saddle. Ridden, she follows `SoraMountRules`: the rider's MoveForward, Sprint and MoveRight set gait and steering, auto-steer keeps her on the road while the rider leaves the reins alone, the road's walk and forced-gait stretches cap her, an opponent entering her fear radius or a spook volume spooks her, and her frame sweeps against walls. The rider sits with his eye 225 cm above the ground and dismounts to her right (her left only against a wall). From the saddle the yumi draws only to the left, the katana cuts and the yari thrusts only in their arcs, and a matchlock cannot be taken up. A convoy walks the road ahead at its pace and waits when its rider is 25 m away; a laden pack mare follows on her lead rope. A mounted fight's rider comes down as its dismounted phase begins, and a ride ends with the rider on the ground.

Cinematics play behind the letterboxed card with their subtitles on the card's clock, through a camera that blends in from the hero's eye and dollies over the beat's place; Enter skips them and the view blends back.

Who takes the field sits behind four small functions for the AI lane to rewire at merge: `SpawnWaveOpponent(Type, …)`, `SpawnStealthGuard(Type, …)`, `SpawnDuelOpponent(Npc, …)` and `SpawnBoss(BossId, …)`. Until then every opponent is the prototype invader, a sparring pupil (bokuto and shinai bouts, training targets) or the collector, and stealth guards fight at once as `on_detected: combat` would.

Failures follow the task: a fall in a sparring match is the rival's point, a lost match or lesson bout restarts in place, the last allowed loss moves the story on, and anything else shows the result screen and returns to the latest checkpoint with Enter. `RaiseBeatEvent(FName)` is how the world reports to the beat: it plays the event's lines, releases the waves it names, and applies the failure it raises (`alert_state:alert` or `:combat` is detection and marks the hero seen, `npc_died:<id>` an escort down, `convoy_member_*` a lost convoy, `target_destroyed:*`, `horse_down`, or the beat's exact `fail` rows such as `boss_event:mill_seal_3`). `DamageDefendedTarget` damages a defended target and fails the beat when it is destroyed. A hit on the hero drops a carried load where the carry says so.

The story HUD (`SoraHUDStoryPanels.cpp`) shows the chapter's title card before its opening cinematic, then the letterboxed frame with the beat title and subtitles. In play, a tracker at the top right names the beat (its number among the chapter's required beats and its title in both languages) and its task: progress with a bar, a hold's seconds and whether the hero has left the ground, a crank slipping, the escorted party waiting, a load being carried, the manufacturing steps done, now and next, each defended target's health, and on a ride the horse's gait (常歩, 速歩, 駈歩, 襲歩), pace and wind, a spook, and what E does (the next tack piece, mounting from her right, dismounting). Beneath it, story notices name each unlocked weapon and mechanic, each find with its codex text and each restored site. The E hints for work, caches and pickups are bilingual.

Dialogue is keyed by events. `FireDialogueTrigger(FName)` plays the rows an event keys in the active beat and the chapter's bark pools, and shows its HUD prompt. The director raises `beat_start`, `beat_complete`, `subtitle`, `boss_phase:N`, `duel_phase:N`, `duel_event:<id>`, `site_restored:N`, `player_died`, `point:player`/`point:rival`, `bout_scored:N`, `duel_won`, `match_lost`, `loss_continue`, `boss_defeated`, `gate_complete:<gate>`, `progress_at:X`, `idle_s:N`, `wave_start:N`, `wave_spawned[:N|:<id>]`, `interact:<id>`, `interact_start`, `item_picked:<id>`, `item_delivered:<id>` (which also releases the waves waiting on it), `item_dropped`, the tack-up's `interact:<piece>`, `interact_refused:<piece>:N` and `item_given:<gift>`, `mount`, `dismount`, `horse_spooked`, `rate_band:in|above|below`, `event:crank_turn_N`, `crank_released`, `step_start:<kind>`, `step_complete:<kind>:<n>`, `step_failed:<kind>`, `output_complete`, `target_hp_below:<actor>:<x>`, `lines_done:<line>`, `exit_reached`, the beat's own volume, interact and closing events, `collectible:<id>`, `collectible_found:<id>` and `chapter_complete`. Lines never repeat unless repeatable; repeatable rows wait out their `cooldown_s`; conditional rows wait for their find or flag. The queue plays each line for its `duration_s`; `QueuedSubtitles()` gives the queue with the speaker's Japanese and English names, the Japanese line and the English subtitle. `USoraGameEvents` announces `OnBeatStarted`, `OnBeatCompleted` and `OnBeatFailed`, and each beat event appends a JSON line (with the beat clock, progress, count done and losses) to `Saved/Telemetry/campaign_beats.jsonl`.

`USoraSaveGame` schema 6 holds the chapter, the reach, the unlocks and story flags held when the chapter began, the 48-bit collectibles mask and the checkpoint: the beat (or boss or duel phase) it starts, the beats done and the sites, weapons, mechanics, story flags and dojo forms they earned, or a finished chapter at its result. `LoadSave` starts a fresh campaign for any other schema (no schema-5 decoder), a chapter beyond the reach, entry unlocks outside `EntryValid`, story flags no earlier chapter sets, or collectibles from an unreached chapter; `ResetRound` replays the chapter from its opening when the checkpoint fails `CheckpointValid`, `ProgressValid` or `TaskCheckpointValid`, or its dojo forms differ from those taught. An optional find is kept at once; its beat's completion and story flag reach the save at the next checkpoint. Chapter advance carries the route's unlocks and flags forward; chapter select grants every unlock and flag the earlier chapters offer; New Campaign clears them. Nyaakoto's equipment refuses a weapon the campaign has not unlocked, with a bilingual notice; the training arena lends every arm.

## In-editor acceptance

`scripts/toolchain/run-automation-mac.sh SoraArena` runs these campaign tests in real game worlds with isolated save slots:

- `SoraArena.Campaign.Playthrough` plays all eight chapters (125 required beats) through Enter, E, the hero's position (or his horse's) and lethal damage, and records how many beats of each task goal it played; every goal the chapters author (14) must be played. Every beat must start with the hero in its place, every cinematic (30) must play through its camera and give the view back, every ride (7) must be ridden, ch01.b06's horse tacked up (six presses: the bit twice refused), refused from her left and mounted from her right, and every rider down at the destination. Each crank's sluice or lift gauge must rise with its first turn, the first load must slow the hero to its pace, show in his arms and refuse the yumi, and chapters one to five pick up every loose collectible (18), which the save keeps at once. It reads the disk save back at every checkpoint beat (99), restores three checkpoints in fresh worlds, and takes the failure paths: two rival points and a lost sparring match retried in place (ch01.b11), an invader standing at a pack horse wearing it down, a repair making it whole, a wounded and destroyed pack horse failing its defence to the checkpoint (ch02.b06), a defeat retried (ch03.b08) and a stealth alarm calling its reinforcements (ch05.b02). A hurried crank turn slips, an off-beat stroke restarts its rhythm, holds fill only with the hero in them, and nothing is worked from afar. It checks the subtitle queue's speakers, the yari and arc carbine staying locked until their beats, chapters one to five opening every cache and six to eight skipping theirs except the flagship's name board, whose story flag is saved and carried into the final chapter, and it ends with all 21 weapons and 19 mechanics and a liberated city that survives a reload.
- `SoraArena.Campaign.Spaces` builds every chapter's space in a real world and checks that a trace down meets a floor at z 0 and a standing cat's capsule fits at every zone entry and road node and at every beat's entry, anchor, points, spawns, patrol posts, cache and horse, every trigger volume, exit and closing anchor, and every loose collectible (2,775 points), that each production site stands on its base, and that the hero begins at the first beat's entry.
- `SoraArena.Campaign.Mount` rides through the player's keys: ch01.b06's tack-up, the refusal from her left and the mount from her right with the rider's eye above her back; W walking her on at 160 cm/s, the lane's walk-only stretch holding her to a walk, Shift trotting her at 360 cm/s on the trotting stretch and never past it, auto-steer keeping her on the road, easing to a walk and a halt, the pheasant spooking her, no weapon in the saddle, and the rider on the ground at the range when the ride ends. At ch02.b08 the yumi draws only to the left and the katana cuts only to the right, the convoy walks at 450 cm/s and waits for a rider far ahead, and the gallop at 850 cm/s winds her into a canter below 20. At ch08.b02 the arc carbine cannot be held in the saddle and the yari thrusts forward. At ch04.b14 Bunta follows on her rope; at ch04.b16 the assessor engine's second phase is fought on foot.
- `SoraArena.Campaign.ForgedSaves` writes three valid checkpoints and fourteen forgeries (including a story flag no earlier chapter sets and an unearned flag at the checkpoint) and checks what a real world restores from each.
