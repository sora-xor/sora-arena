# Nyaagyu horse and tack source study

`Nyaagyu_HorseAndTack.blend` is an editable original static asset. `SM_Nyaagyu_HorseAndTack.fbx` is a single-mesh Unreal import candidate, and `Nyaagyu_HorseAndTack_Blender.png` is a Blender review render. The generator records dimensions, hashes, source components, and independent FBX import checks in `manifest.json`.

The model faces +X, stands on Z=0, and uses meters. It depicts a compact brown Japanese-type mount with a raised wood-and-lacquer **kura** (saddle), a separate cloth **aori** on each flank, a girth, breaststrap, crupper, matched broad **abumi** (stirrups), an **omogai** (headstall) and iron **hami** (bit), and paired leather **tazuna** (reins). Reins terminate in named empty hand anchors rather than being pulled taut by an absent rider. The six empties in the `.blend` also identify the seat and feet for later rigging; the FBX is intentionally static. The SORA mark on each saddlecloth and stirrup uses both exact paths of the user-supplied SVG and records its SHA-256. The fictional resistance mon is not attributed to a historical clan.

## Period evidence and interpretation

- The [Met's late-eighteenth-century Japanese horse trappings](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/22586) include a lacquered saddle, silver-inlaid stirrups, and chest/flank ceremonial braids. This is the period/material reference for the ensemble. The model simplifies its straps and omits ceremonial braids; it is not a copy of that accession.
- The [Met's probably eighteenth-century pair of abumi by Sanemitsu](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/712927) was made in Kashū (now Kanazawa, Ishikawa). The museum names the **hato mune** (pigeon-breast) form and reports individual dimensions near **27 × 13 × 25 cm**; the modeled foot enclosure takes those dimensions as its target. Its wood tread, iron shell, lacquer-like finish, and silver details are an interpretation, rather than a measurement-based scan.
- The [Met's late-sixteenth/early-seventeenth-century abumi pair](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/35376) displays a prominent Tokugawa mon on the front. This supports locating the game's SORA mon on the front of its abumi. That accession predates the modeled late-Edo-inspired ensemble, and the SORA emblem is intentionally fictional.
- The [Met's 1491 kura later relacquered in the seventeenth century](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/40468) documents a preserved Japanese saddle with a wood, high-front/high-rear silhouette and later Edo lacquer decoration. Our maewa and shizuwa echo that silhouette but are not a reproduction of the 1491 object.
- The [Met's mounted-samurai ensemble dated eighteenth–nineteenth century](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/35729) includes a lacquered saddle, silver-inlaid stirrups, and an iron bit signed by Myōchin Sadahisa. The [Kyoto National Museum's explanatory horse-tack diagram](https://www.kyohaku.go.jp/jp/learn/home/dictio/kouko/245/) explains how a bit communicates rein movements and names the headstall/bit/tazuna chain; that diagram specifically concerns **Kofun-period** horse gear and is used only for terminology and basic connection, not as direct evidence of Edo strap ornament.
- [Nagoya University Museum's account of the last purebred Kiso horse](https://www.num.nagoya-u.ac.jp/media/ouchi/column_000487.html) records 132 cm at the withers. The model's ~1.32 m withers uses that value as a compact scale cue; it does not claim to reconstruct a historical Edo horse or genetically represent a Kiso horse.

Use `blender -b --factory-startup --python scripts/art/create_nyaagyu_horse_tack.py` to regenerate, then `blender -b Content/SourceArt/Horses/Nyaagyu/Nyaagyu_HorseAndTack.blend --python scripts/art/validate_nyaagyu_horse_tack.py` to round-trip check the FBX. The validator checks the paired stirrups and reins, saddle tree, bit, mon provenance, named anchors, source/FBX bounds, mesh counts, and the material slots.

This remains a **source-art study**. The horse's silhouette, joint transitions, hoof shape, tack fit, and materials require a production sculpt and expert equestrian review. No rig, skin weights, horse gait or riding animation, rein tension, saddle-contact deformation, gameplay collision, Unreal rendering validation, or photoreal production quality is claimed. The decorative SORA mon is explicitly a fictional substitution. No paid or third-party horse geometry was used.
